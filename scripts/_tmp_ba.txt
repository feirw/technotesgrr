The University of the Aegean  
 
1.1. Establishment & Development 
 
The University of the Aegean was founded on March 20, 1984 under the Presidential 
Decree 83/1984, making real the vision of the great Greek Mathematician C. 
Caratheodori. The University of the Aegean is an innovative institution in that it is 
dispersed over si x islands in the Aegean Archipelago and its schools and departments 
offer study opportunities and interdisciplinary programs, catering to the complexity of 
current scientific knowledge. 
 
The academic goal of the University is to develop new directions in t he field of Higher 
Education, responding to the evolving demands of the modern world. Fields of studies 
such as the Environment, the Social Sciences, Business Sciences, the Natural Sciences, 
Information Technologies and Communications, Humanities, Greek and Mediterranean 
Studies constitute the main areas of focus. . 
 
In addition to providing modern education, the University focuses its activities on 
promoting and fostering basic and applied research with well-equipped laboratories. At 
the same time, the Uni versity attempts to contribute to the development of Greece as a 
whole and to the Aegean region in particular, turning the Aegean islands into a center of 
excellence in science and education. 
 
Today, the University of the Aegean comprises 6 Schools, 18 Bac helor programs and 51 
Postgraduate Study Programs, all operating on the islands of Lemnos, Lesvos, Chios, 
Samos, Rhodes and Syros. The university also engages in significant research projects. 
 
For more information visit: www.aegean.gr. 
 
 
1.2 The School of Management Sciences (Business School) 
  
The Business School has been operating in Chios since 1998, comprising the following 
departments: 
 
• Business Administration

The aim of the Business School is the study and analysis of the external and internal 
environment of business enterprises, ensuring the search for, selection and application of 
the most appropriate and effective responses in business challenges. This aim is pursued

2.1 Department of Business Administration (DBA) 
 
The Department of Business Administration was the first department of the University of 
the Aegean to admit students at undergraduate level in the fall semester of the 1985 - 86 
academic year. To date, the Department has awarded 2620 Bachelor’s degrees, 1245

The curriculum allows students to select modules according to their personal preferences 
and career orientation. The curriculum’s streams are:

3.1. Laboratories in the Department of Business Administration 
 
 Quantitative Methods Laboratory (QM Lab) 
Director: Professor Maria Mavri 
 
The Laboratory of Quantitative Methods was established in September 2003. Its main 
objective is research and development of methods and techniques that contribute to the 
efficient management of modern organizations, operating in a changing business 
environment. 
 
Members of the Lab are: 
• Quantitative Methods Faculty (Mathematics, Statistics, Operational Research). 
• Adjuncts and PhD candidates, whose experience and research interests are related to 
quantitative methods and their applications. 
 
The Laboratory also cooperates with faculty members not only of the DBA but also of the 
other Departments of the School of Management Sciences, whose fields of study fall 
within the interests of Business Administration, Finance, Finance, Information 
Technology and Communications. 
 
The Laboratory of Quantitative Methods collaborates with universities, organizations and 
research institutes, aiming at the transfer and diffusion of know -how and research on 
issues of common interest. 
The research fields of the Laboratory are the following: 
 
Statistical analysis 
 
This field focuses on the design of statistical research, sampling, database creation, 
statistical data analysis and statistical inference. In addition to research the field is also a 
source of specialized information for the world of business. 
 
Decision making 
 
This field focuses on the decision -making process on issues relating to business 
management, as well as on knowledge, innovation and quality management. The Lab

uses state-of-the-art methodologies and techniques. At the same time, it develops and 
implements its own methodologies case -specific, and always aims at improving the 
quality of business decisions taken and at developing integrated decision support 
systems. 
 
Regional Analysis 
 
The regional problem, namely the uneven development of the various regions of the 
country and the difficulties faced by isolated and insular regions, is of great research 
interest to the Lab. The main purpose of this field is to determine the level of development 
of a geographical area and its future prospects, with an emphasis on island regions. The 
Lab has developed a methodology for calculating the attractiveness of a geographical area 
and its development potential.  
The Lab has engaged in extensive research and has participated in several European and 
national programs. During this period, more than 20 papers have been published in 
international scientific journals, while approximately 30 papers are in progress, some of 
which have been presented at inter national and national conferences. The Lab has 
participated in the organization of two international and one national conferences. 
 
In short, the research activity conducted in the Lab enables new scientists belonging to 
various fields of study to collaborate and solve business management problems.

 Career Days.

Internships    may    count    as    a n    elective    course.    The internship program is

 1st quarter: January 1 – March 31 
 2nd quarter: April 1 – June 30

6. D.B.A.: Operation and Staff 
 
 Historical Background 
The Department of Business Administration was the first department of the University 
of the Aegean to admit students and has been operating for 40 years.  Its official opening 
took place on October 30, 1985 (Decree 83/1984). The Department began operating in 
the Fall of 1985 with seven contract faculty members, seven administrative employees 
and 103 newly admitted students. 
Four years later, the Department awarded its first ten Bachelor’s degrees.  
Mr. Stergios Babanasis was the first professor to be declared emeritus (in the year 2003). 
 
The Department’s innovative programs and practices have often been adopted by other 
departments of the University and by other Greek universities. Among them are: 
 
 Summer Internship Program (1986). 
 Liaison Committee (1990), 
 Field trips to companies in Greece and abroad           Weekly guest lectures 
 Student Counseling (1985)

 The Aims of the Curriculum are the following: 
 The integration of new data and trends, both academically - nationally and 
internationally, and more broadly at social, economic and business level, as regards 
the concepts of Entrepreneurship, Innovation, Sustainable Development, Digital 
Transformation. 
 The increase in flexibility of the curriculum with the establishment of 6 Specializations

the Core Courses to 28 from 34, while at the same time increasing the Specialization 
courses to 8 instead of 6 and the Electives to 12 instead of 8

as regards the improvement of the General Knowledge Courses Index and the Skills 
Development Courses Index.

C. EXAMINATION REGULATIONS 
 
Introduction 
 
Exam regulations are necessary for maintaining the integrity of the examination process. 
 
Exam Preparation 
 
 The Department Senate sets the dates of the exam period. 
 The detailed examination schedule for  fall      and spring semesters is announced at 
least 30 days before exams. 
 The detailed schedule for the examination period in September is announced by the 
end of the spring exam period. 
 The detailed schedule for exam supervision is sent to teaching and supervisory staff 
15 days prior to the examination period. 
 
Before the Exam 
 
 The examination rooms are open 15 minutes before the start of the exam. 
 Each examinee is expected to be present at least 15 minutes before the start of the 
exam. 
 Exam supervisors are required to be present at the Secretariat of the Department at 
least 15 minutes before the start of the exam. 
 Supervisors are responsible for the seating arrangement of examinees as well as the 
integrity of the examination process. 
 Examinees are requir ed to remove from their desks all books or any other material 
relevant to the exam. Mobile phones or any other electronic devices are not allowed 
during exams, unless approved by the teaching staff. 
 Exam cancelations and exam rescheduling are decided upon by the Department 
Senate, or the Department Head. 
 Exam supervision is conducted by the teaching staff and other university adjuncts 
such as PhD students, university fellows or post-PhD students. 
 
Examination Results 
 
Grades are submitted within ten (10) days after the exam period. 
Once the grades are announced, students may ask to see their marked exam up to two 
months after exam results have been issued. Exam papers are kept until their disposal is 
decided upon. 
 
An exam archive may be available at the Library.

Duties and Obligations of Faculty- Examiners 
 
 Before exams faculty examiners must announce the following: 
 The examinable material 
 Bibliographic references 
 Exam type (oral, written, etc.) 
 
The monitoring of the examinations is primarily the responsibility of the faculty. Faculty 
members must be present during exams to make clarifications and answer questions 
students may ask. 
If for any reason the examiner is absent on the day of the exam, the Department Head 
may ask another examiner to administer the exam. 
 
Duties and Obligations of Examinees 
 
Students must carry their ID card and present it upon request. 
Students are responsible to conduct themselves appropriately and follow the instructions 
of exam supervisors, otherwise they may be expelled and penalized. They are not allowed 
to participate in an exam when they arrive 15 minutes after the commencement of the 
exam. Latecomers are not allowed extra time, and students cannot leave the examination 
room for the first 15 minutes of the exam. Students who must leave the room temporarily 
may do so only if accompanied. Exam papers cannot be taken out of the examination 
room without approval. Certificates of exam attendance are issued upon request. 
Students who are physically challenged may be given alternative forms of examination. 
 
 Upholding Regulations and Exam Integrity 
 
The Department Senate is responsible for upholding exam regulations and imposing 
sanctions when they are violated. Violations are considered by the Department Senate 
after the end of the exam period and before results are announced.
